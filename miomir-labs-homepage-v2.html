Why we exist

Nigeria does not have a shortage of people who can ship a product. It has a shortage of systems that survive their second year.

Ask around and the same failures repeat. A balance stored in a column instead of derived from entries, and now nobody can prove what a customer is owed. A schema built for one merchant, discovered a year later to need every table migrated. A business that stops when one bank's API changes, because the architecture assumed the partnership would hold forever. A handover that leaves a team unable to change their own product.

None of that happens because the engineers were poor. It happens because almost nobody in this market is paid to build for year five. Launch pressure is real, capital is short, and the incentive is to get something on screen and worry later. Later arrives, and it is expensive.

We exist to be the house that builds for later.

That means we work on the layer where mistakes compound: ledgers, identity, records, the movement of money. It means we take fewer engagements than we could sell. And it means we run our own products on the same foundations we sell, so that when we tell you a decision will hurt in year two, we are speaking from having been hurt by it.

What we do

Three kinds of work. The same people do all of them.

Client engineering funds the house. The products keep us honest about what it is like to operate the thing after launch. The research is how we stay useful in five years.

Client engineering

Systems of record

Research
